Observing Locomotion Patterns of Metazygia wilfradae in Light and Dark Conditions

Neuroscience 2023, Washington, D.C.

When it was unclear whether spiders exhibit circadian rhythms independent of external light cues, I designed and ran a 14-day observational study to find out. I collected and cleaned 10,000+ locomotion observations from 30 spiders in Python, then built Matplotlib/Seaborn visualizations to detect rhythmic patterns across light and dark conditions. The result: circadian periods of 16–32 hours that persisted independent of light exposure — findings accepted for a poster presentation at the Society for Neuroscience 2023 national conference, and proof of the same design-clean-model-communicate workflow required in commercial analytics roles.

Predicting Engineer Attrition Risk in an AI-Disrupted Workplace

BUS 306 – Regression Analysis, Wake Forest University, 2026

When Snowflake Inc.'s engineering leadership worried that accelerating AI adoption was fueling employee attrition, our three-person consulting team set out to find out why. We analyzed survey data from 719 tech workers, building a logistic regression model to test whether fear of AI replacement, burnout, and job satisfaction predicted attrition risk, then a second model to test whether AI usage itself was driving that fear. The result: fear of AI replacement, burnout, and low job satisfaction were all statistically significant predictors of attrition risk (p < 0.001, pseudo R² = 0.51) — but AI usage metrics were not, meaning perception, not exposure, is the real risk. We delivered Snowflake a set of actionable HR recommendations: track AI sentiment (not just adoption) in employee surveys, communicate clearly what AI will and won't replace before rollouts, and cap new AI tool introductions per quarter to reduce disruption.

What Drives Airbnb Listing Availability Across NYC Boroughs?

BUS 306 – Regression Analysis, Wake Forest University, 2026

Airbnb's internal analytics team (our simulated client) wanted to understand which listing-level factors predict how many nights a property sits unbooked, to guide host recruiting and coaching materials across New York City. Our team cleaned and modeled 40,000+ NYC listings, running OLS regressions citywide and separately within each borough to compare how policies like minimum-night requirements, review frequency, and host listing count affect annual availability. Minimum-night requirements emerged as a consistent, statistically significant driver of availability citywide, but the strength of that relationship varied widely by borough — with much lower explanatory power in smaller markets. We recommended Airbnb tailor host coaching on minimum-stay policy by borough rather than apply one blanket citywide guideline, and flagged the need for richer listing-quality data to improve predictive power in future models.

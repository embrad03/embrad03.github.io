Observing Locomotion Patterns of Metazygia wilfradae in Light and Dark Conditions

Neuroscience 2023, Washington, D.C.

My role: Independent researcher — I designed the study, collected and cleaned the data, and built the analysis and visualizations myself, start to finish. When it was unclear whether spiders exhibit circadian rhythms independent of external light cues, I designed and ran a 14-day observational study to find out. Predicting Engineer Attrition Risk in an AI-Disrupted Workplace

BUS 306 – Regression Analysis, Washington and Lee University, 2026 · Simulated client case study

My role: Core team member on a three-person team — I contributed across data prep, modeling, and the client-facing recommendations rather than owning a single stage alone. For a regression analysis course at Washington and Lee University, my team took on the role of an external consulting group advising a simulated client — a fictional AI-adoption scenario at Snowflake Inc. — on rising engineering attrition. We analyzed survey data from 719 tech workers, building a logistic regression model to test whether fear of AI replacement, burnout, and job satisfaction predicted attrition risk, then a second model to test whether AI usage itself was driving that fear. The result: fear of AI replacement, burnout, and low job satisfaction were all statistically significant predictors of attrition risk (p < 0.001, pseudo R² = 0.51) — but AI usage metrics were not, meaning perception, not exposure, is the real risk. We delivered our simulated client a set of actionable HR recommendations: track AI sentiment (not just adoption) in employee surveys, communicate clearly what AI will and won't replace before rollouts, and cap new AI tool introductions per quarter to reduce disruption. That core finding — that perceived risk drives behavior more than actual exposure — maps directly onto healthcare analytics, where patient and provider trust in a model or new technology often matters as much as its clinical accuracy.

What Drives Airbnb Listing Availability Across NYC Boroughs?

BUS 306 – Regression Analysis, Washington and Lee University, 2026 · Simulated role-play exercise

My role: Core team member — I worked across data cleaning, the borough-level regression modeling, and the final recommendations alongside my teammates. For the same course, my team took on the fictional role of Airbnb's internal analytics team, tasked with a simulated assignment: understand which listing-level factors predict how many nights a property sits unbooked, to guide host recruiting and coaching materials across New York City. We cleaned and modeled 40,000+ NYC listings, running OLS regressions citywide and separately within each borough to compare how policies like minimum-night requirements, review frequency, and host listing count affect annual availability. Minimum-night requirements emerged as a consistent, statistically significant driver of availability citywide, but the strength of that relationship varied widely by borough — with much lower explanatory power in smaller markets. We recommended tailoring host coaching on minimum-stay policy by borough rather than applying one blanket citywide guideline, and flagged the need for richer listing-quality data to improve predictive power in future models. The same lesson — that a single national model can mask meaningful regional variation — is central to healthcare analytics, where treatment and utilization patterns often differ sharply across facilities or patient populations.
